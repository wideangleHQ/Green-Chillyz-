# 05 — Visual Direction

## Moodboard / Art Direction
Cinematic Organic: think architectural fine-dining editorial meets tech-product minimalism. References: Apple product pages (restraint, single focal subject), Awwwards F&B sites (motion craft), high-end restaurant photography (moody, ingredient-forward).

## Photography
- Food: macro, shallow depth of field, natural + warm artificial light mix, steam/texture emphasized.
- Camera angles: 45° hero shots for dishes, top-down for spread/ingredient shots, eye-level for people/ambience.
- Lighting: warm-neutral, avoid cold blue-white; shadows soft, never harsh flash-lit.
- Composition: generous negative space around subject to allow glass UI overlay without occlusion of the focal point.

## Backgrounds
- Base: warm neutral (#FFF8F0), never pure white.
- Organic blob shapes: primary-green/lime, 100px+ Gaussian blur, positioned off-center, low opacity (10–20%) so they read as ambient light, not shapes.
- Gradients: soft linear (primary→dark green) for CTA/button surfaces only; radial low-opacity lime blur for ambient background only.

## Glassmorphism Application
Glass panels sit over photography/gradients, never over another glass panel. Blur 16–32px, border 1px white/20% opacity — consistent across every section.

## 3D Asset Direction
If used, 3D elements (e.g., coin/reward iconography) should be soft-shaded, low-poly-avoidant (smooth organic forms), matte-not-glossy, matching the organic shape language — no hard-surface/sci-fi rendering.

## Food Styling
Fresh, vibrant, minimal props — let the dish be the subject. No cluttered tablescapes.

## People
Candid, warm, genuine expressions; diverse representation of GreenChillyz Group's customer base. No stiff corporate stock-photo posing.

## Environment
Outlet interiors shown selectively (Locations/Franchise sections) — clean, modern-casual, brand-colored accents visible but not overwhelming.

## Brand Mood Summary
Premium but approachable. Confident, not flashy. Warm, not saccharine. Every image should feel intentional enough to belong in an editorial food magazine.
